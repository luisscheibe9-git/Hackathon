# Camera-free people tracking and fall detection

Research reviewed November 13, 2026, for the Cal Poly AI Hackathon.

## What we should build

**Start with one person in one room. If a TI IWR6843AOPEVM is available, use it to track position and height, and use the ESP32 CSI as an independent motion signal.** A rapid drop in measured height followed by a sustained low posture is a stronger basis for a fall alert than a spike in Wi-Fi motion. Draw the radar track on the floor plan and attach the last reliable zone to the event. TI already supplies 3D people tracking and a fall demonstration based on height change. [TI tracking reference design](https://www.ti.com/tool/TIDEP-01000) · [TI fall and stance design](https://www.ti.com/lit/ta/sszt417/sszt417.pdf)

| Hardware we can get working in time | What to build |
| --- | --- |
| **TI IWR6843AOPEVM** plus ESP32 | Best single-sensor option here for tracking plus a fall signal. Run TI's tracker first and feed its position, height and timestamps to the laptop. Add CSI in parallel and measure whether it improves the result. |
| **Seeed MR60FDA2** plus ESP32 | Quickest dedicated fall input if we can mount it overhead. Use standard ESPHome presence/fall outputs and label the configured zone. Verify sit, controlled floor descent and recovery under the actual mount. For live x/y on the map, use a separate tracker or verify access to the required data before committing to it. |
| **Hi-Link LD2450** plus ESP32 | Good for a live x/y display of moving targets. It reports x/y and speed, so use it to attach a zone to a CSI candidate. Use a height-capable sensor for fall confirmation. |
| **Three ESP32s already on hand** | Use multi-link CSI for one person's motion and estimated zone. Show “possible fall” for a locally trained event classifier, with “unknown” when coverage or zone confidence is poor. |

[TI tracking lab](https://dev.ti.com/tirex/explore/content/radar_toolbox_4_00_00_05/source/ti/examples/Industrial_and_Personal_Electronics/Vital_Signs/Vital_Signs_With_People_Tracking/docs/vital_signs_with_people_tracking_user_guide.html) · [Seeed kit](https://wiki.seeedstudio.com/getting_started_with_mr60fda2_mmwave_kit/) · [ESPHome outputs](https://esphome.io/components/seeed_mr60fda2/) · [Hi-Link serial spec](https://hlktech.com/Goods-352.html)

Two other add-ons are worth a bench test if already available. A downward-facing **VL53L5CX time-of-flight module** can measure coarse height over a small unobstructed patch with an 8×8 distance grid. This is a candidate for checking out-of-bed or floor posture in a fixed zone. A **floor vibration sensor** can add impact timing, as in the ESP32-C3 mmHome prototype. Treat impact as supporting evidence because soft slides and dropped objects can undermine a hard impact rule. [ST time-of-flight sensor](https://www.st.com/en/imaging-and-photonics-solutions/vl53l5cx.html) · [mmHome radar and vibration prototype](https://github.com/nathanwuzhao/mmHome)

## What the experiments say

| Result | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| **Two ESP32s caught 14 of 16 falls** and flagged none of 13 walking trials in a 2026 preprint. The boards were 3.5 m apart, and the classifier used amplitude, wavelet features and LogitBoost. [Preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.15532v1#S4) | A feasible starting point. The evaluation is small and compares falls with walking. Fast sitting, intentional lying, room transfer and live false alerts remain open. |
| **A 2023 hardware comparison found noisy ESP32 CSI.** The authors reported >96% on their larger dataset, after choosing a different capture setup. [Chu et al., Sections II–IV](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/202278/8/Deep_Learning_Based_Fall_Detection_Using_WiFi_Channel_State_Information.pdf) | Validate packet quality and signal separation on our hardware before investing in a classifier. |
| **XFall** used five volunteers, three environments, 1,000 staged falls and 2,800 daily activities. Its cross-environment chart shows roughly 8% missed alarms and 7% false alarms. [XFall, Table I and Fig. 11](https://tns.thss.tsinghua.edu.cn/~guoxuan/assets/pdf/Paper-XFall.pdf) | A strong precedent for testing transfer and hard negatives. Its 5.825 GHz 2×2 MIMO routers, 350 packets/sec and video supervision differ from our setup. |
| **Six-ESP32-S3 csi-pose project** reports 10 of 11 staged falls with two false positives on one replay session. It used webcam-derived pose labels and height/pose rules. A standalone CSI stillness check was disabled after the team found overlapping motion readings across postures. [csi-pose implementation and limits](https://github.com/sel00000/csi-pose) | An example of how far a tuned demo can go, and why stillness alone is weak evidence. |
| **Four-radar study** reported 97.9% in staged multi-person tests. When the team first applied the same threshold to fall-like daily activities, accuracy dropped to 76.8%. [Study, Table 5 and Figures 7–8](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12988233/) | Include deep squats, fast sitting, bending and soft falls before trusting the radar. Multiple people increased missed detections. |
| **Long-term in-home radar field study** reported about one false alarm per day at 90% detection at one operating point. [Skubic et al.](https://c2ship.missouri.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Skubic-Fall-detection-Final.pdf) | Record false alerts per hour of ordinary use. A high score on a balanced set of staged clips can hide a disruptive alert rate. |

## Build and test in this order

1. **Get one clean link.** On the classic ESP32-WROOM-32U, attach its 2.4 GHz U.FL antenna. Use Espressif's `csi_recv_router` with a controlled 2.4 GHz router for a quick trace, then `csi_send` and `csi_recv` for a dedicated sender and two receivers. Put the receivers at different angles. Fix the channel and mounts, filter to our transmitter, and send CSI to the laptop over USB serial. Start around 50 received frames/sec per link as a practical motion baseline, then measure actual rate and gaps before deciding whether higher rates improve the fall signal. [Espressif examples](https://github.com/espressif/esp-csi/tree/master/examples/get-started) · [Better Stack packet setup](https://github.com/andrisgauracs/wifi-sensing-kit/blob/main/docs/how-it-works.md)
2. **Make the sensor readings visible.** Log timestamp, link ID, packet rate and amplitude per usable subcarrier. Plot empty, walk, fast sit and controlled descent. Baseline each link, reject malformed frames and spikes, and record missing data. Use short-window amplitude variability, event duration and change across links for the first classifier. Compare a threshold baseline against a small feature-based model if there are enough labeled trials. Calibrate zone labels on taped floor areas and let the output abstain. [Wi-Fi sensing kit features](https://github.com/andrisgauracs/wifi-sensing-kit/blob/main/docs/how-it-works.md) · [Two-board preprocessing](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.15532v1#S3)
3. **Keep the signals independent in the first test.** With a 3D radar, start with rapid descent plus a low track that persists. With an MR60FDA2, use its built-in fall and presence events. Record CSI around the event and compare radar alone, CSI alone and a combined rule on identical recordings. Avoid a mandatory CSI veto unless the comparison supports it, especially around soft falls or gaps in CSI. Hold an alert through stillness or brief track loss and distinguish recovery, manual dismissal and sensor dropout.
4. **Run a small blind demo test.** Tune on one recording session. Test on a later session and, if possible, a second person and one changed layout. Keep all windows from an event in the same split. Compare sideways/forward controlled descents and slow slides with ordinary walking, fast sitting, kneeling, floor exercise or intentional lying, bending to pick up an object and a dropped object. Use a mat and controlled movements. Label time and zone from an observer, so the project can stay camera-free throughout.
5. **Report event counts.** Put staged falls detected out of total, false alerts during ordinary activity and its duration, median alert delay, zone correctness and the number of unknown zones on one demo slide. If showing live x/y, mark floor points and report median and worst-case or 90th-percentile error. With multiple sensors, run the same playback with each sensor alone so the audience can see what each one contributed.

First milestone: live CSI from the existing board and a chart that separates empty from walking. In parallel, check whether a height-capable radar is available. Demo milestone: a person walks and sits without an alert, a controlled descent raises one, and the dashboard retains the event time and last reliable zone when movement stops. Exact pose and multi-person tracking can follow after this works.
